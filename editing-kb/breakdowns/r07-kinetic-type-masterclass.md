# r07: "Triangle of editing" kinetic-type masterclass (16:9, 24 fps, ~29 s)

**Concept:** a talking-head lesson in which *every spoken word becomes a visual*. 61 soft cuts in 29 s. Average shot ≈ 0.48 s, and in the 5.2–7.2 s burst a new frame arrives every 2 frames (≈ 0.08 s).
**Audio:** VO at about -20 LUFS integrated (quiet; short-form usually wants -14), with a soft music bed. Many small high-band ticks/swishes (on nearly every word) and **sub hits at 14.1, 14.7 and 14.9 s** under "hit those moments": the sound literally does what the words say.

## The core rule: word = beat = visual change
The visual changes *on the word*, not on the sentence. Transcript timings vs cuts:
- "Sound,"@4.50 → giant "SOUND" wraps around his face (4.6)
- "pacing,"@5.44 → "PACING" in red over a desk shot (5.3)
- "and motion."@5.96–6.12 → "AND / MOTION." over a burst of 2-frame flashes (5.6–7.2)
- "let me break it down"@6.6–7.2 → one word per shot ("let", "break", "it") over a pen-tip macro
- "Contrast equals rhythm"@11.5–12.4 → three full-frame giant words, each a hard cut

## Style system
- **Palette:** brand red #A3120F/#C0141B, white, black, plus a blue-lit (#1E5BB0) and a warm tungsten B-roll set. Every graphic uses only red, white or black.
- **Type:** 1) ultra-condensed heavy sans (Anton / Bebas Neue / "Druk"-like) for big words; 2) italic serif (Playfair Display Italic / Instrument Serif) for soft words; 3) rounded heavy grunge display (closest: "Bangers"/"Rubik Dirt") for playful words. Two to three fonts per frame at most.
- **Scale contrast:** giant words fill 30–100% of the frame height; there are no mid-sized captions.
- **Shot variety:** studio white backdrop, tungsten desk B-roll, blue-lit profile, top-down, macro. The changing *light colour* is part of the rhythm.

## Why it feels pro (and what an amateur version gets wrong)
1. Cuts and text land on the **exact word onset** (±1 frame). Amateur: captions follow sentences.
2. **Big contrast between beats**: fast 2-frame strobes, then a 1.5 s held shot (14.8–17.5). Amateur: uniform pace.
3. **Depth layering** (text behind the person, collage over the text). Amateur: text always on top.
4. **Strict palette** (red/white/black). Amateur: many colours.
5. **The sound mirrors the meaning** (sub hits on "hit those moments"). Amateur: generic whooshes.
6. Each layout is used once or twice only, then a new one. Amateur: the same caption template for 30 s.

## Apply to the Mark Cuban edit
- Giant red/gold word *behind* Mark on key nouns: "EVERYTHING", "FIRED", "$1,500", "NO".
- 2-frame strobe montage under a list ("windows, doors, conservatories, double glazing").
- Face/cut-out collage for "SH*THOLE" or "GOT HIM FIRED".
- A sub hit when he says "fired" and when "NO." lands.
